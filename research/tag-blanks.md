# Laser-Engravable Pet Tag Blanks: Amazon Shortlist and Starter Order
Researched 2026-10-11 by Doc. Prices, ratings, and stock were read from live Amazon listings on that date and will drift. Every ASIN below was opened and confirmed; where a figure was not visible it says so.

## Bottom line
- Anodized aluminum blanks on Amazon run $0.16 to $0.54 per tag in packs of 30 to 100, well under the $1.50 target. Stainless bone blanks run $0.80 to $0.89 per tag, well under the $3 target. Blank cost is 1 to 6 percent of a $12 to $16 retail price, so pricing holds with room to spare.
- The best-reviewed laser-specific listings are the Stark Industries military tags (254 ratings, confirmed white engraving on a 40 W CO2) and the Frienda 40-piece shape mix (299 ratings, "engrave great and are a good money maker"). The cheapest reliable bone source is the EupHHonycs 100-pack, where a reviewer confirmed clean results on 2.5 W and 6 W diode lasers.
- Two listings that look attractive by title should be skipped (Frienda 60-piece and the StayMax black bone 20-pack): one has a flake-off complaint and both are out of stock. Details in section 4.
- Nearly every pack ships WITHOUT split rings, and the ones that include rings get ring-quality complaints. Budget a separate ring pack.

## Laser notes that drive the picks
- Anodized aluminum: a diode laser ablates the dye layer and exposes bare aluminum, so the mark reads white/silver. Dark colors (black, navy, red, purple, green) give the strongest contrast. Light pink, yellow, and silver give weak contrast; two separate reviewers flagged pink specifically. Stock dark colors for sale, light colors for accents only.
- "Powder coated" or "painted" aluminum (several of the cheap 100-packs describe themselves this way, and reviewers on two "anodized" listings said they "look painted") lasers the same way but can chip at the edge over time. Fine for a $12 novelty tag, not for a "lifetime" claim.
- Stainless steel: a blue diode will not mark bare stainless on its own. Use a marking spray (xTool's own stainless listings say "black laser marking spray recommended for blue-light lasers") or an IR/fiber module, which marks dark directly. One StayMax reviewer using an xTool F1 (IR) got a dark mark at 100 percent power.
- Thickness: 1.0 mm (0.039 to 0.04 in) is the standard for both aluminum and stainless blanks and is what every pick below uses unless noted. 2 mm stainless (SUPERFINDINGS) feels noticeably more premium in hand. Anything under 0.8 mm bends on a collar.
- Brass: solid brass marks well with fiber/IR and with marking spray on diode. No brass BONE blanks exist on Amazon; only rounds and rectangles.

## 7. Owner's laser: 60 W MOPA fiber (confirmed 2026-10-11)

A 60 W MOPA fiber marks every blank in this file directly, no spray: anodized aluminum (strip to silver, or black-mark on top of the color), bare 304 stainless (black, white, deep engrave), solid brass, titanium. MOPA's party trick is **color marking on stainless and titanium** (blues, golds, purples, reds by pulse width and frequency), which no CO2 or plain Q-switched fiber shop can match: a colored-engraving stainless tag is a real premium SKU. Thickness matters only for deep engraving; 1 mm aluminum is fine for surface marks. Keep stainless/brass at +$4 and consider a "+$6 color-engraved stainless" tier once settings are dialed. Acrylic/wood keychains are NOT a fiber job (fiber passes through clear acrylic); that stays a 3D-print or CO2 category.
