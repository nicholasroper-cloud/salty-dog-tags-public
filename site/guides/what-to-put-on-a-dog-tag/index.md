# What to Put on a Dog Tag (Without Wasting Precious Tag Real Estate)

> Phone number, medical notes, the name debate. Here's what to put on a dog tag, what to skip, and how to make it funny without making it useless.

## The Non-Negotiable: Your Phone Number

If you put only one thing on your dog's tag, make it a phone number. Not your email, not your Instagram handle, not a poem. A phone number.

Here's why: the person who finds your dog is probably standing outside, maybe in the heat, maybe in the rain, maybe on a Florida beach while your bulldog sits on their towel like he paid for it. They want to make one call and be done. Make that easy.

A few tips:

- **Use your cell, not a landline.** You want the number that rings when you're out frantically driving around the neighborhood.
- **Include the area code.** Always. Your dog might wander farther than you think, and a stranger shouldn't have to guess.
- **Add a second number if you have room.** A partner, a roommate, a trusted neighbor. If you're on a plane or in a dead zone, someone else can pick up.
- **Keep it current.** A tag with a disconnected number is just jewelry. If you change numbers, change the tag.

## Should You Put Your Dog's Name on the Tag?

This one gets debated. Some people argue that putting your dog's name on the tag lets a stranger call your dog by name, which could make it easier for a bad actor to win the dog over. Others say a name helps a good Samaritan calm a scared dog and get it to come closer.

Both sides have a point. Here's how we think about it:

- If your dog is friendly with everyone (looking at most bulldogs), the name probably doesn't change much. They'll go with anyone holding a cheese stick.
- If your dog is nervous or skittish, hearing their name from a stranger might help them settle.
- If you're worried about theft, you can leave the name off and use that space for a second phone number or a "microchipped" note instead.

There's no wrong answer. It's your dog and your call. Just don't let the name take up space your phone number needs.

## "Microchipped": Two Words That Pull Their Weight

If your dog has a microchip, say so on the tag. It tells the finder there's a backup plan: any vet or shelter can scan the dog and pull up your info. It also gently signals that this dog is somebody's, and somebody is looking.

Our [sayings list](https://saltydogtags.com/sayings/) has a tag that says it best:

**Microchipped.Still dumb.**

It's useful information delivered with honesty. Your dog's intelligence is between you and your dog.

And a reminder: a microchip only works if the registration is current. Getting the chip is step one. Updating your contact info with the chip registry when you move or change numbers is step two, and a lot of people forget step two.

## Medical Needs and Special Info

If your dog has a condition a stranger *needs* to know about, the tag is the place for it. Short and clear:

- "Needs meds"
- "Diabetic"
- "Deaf"
- "Blind"
- "Allergic to [whatever]"
- "Seizures, call ASAP"

This kind of line can change how someone handles your dog in the hour before you get there. A deaf dog won't respond to being called. A dog who needs medication on a schedule turns a lost-dog situation into an urgent one. Say it plainly. This is not the line for jokes.

Bulldog owners, you know the drill. Our dogs have opinions about heat and stairs, and a squished face that doesn't love a lot of exertion. If your dog has breathing issues or overheats easily, something like "Keep me cool" can actually help a well-meaning stranger, especially in a Florida summer.

## Address: Full, Partial, or Skip It?

A full street address used to be standard on dog tags. These days, a lot of people skip it. Your phone number does the job, and some folks just aren't comfortable putting their home address on something that dangles in public.

Middle-ground options:

- **City only.** Helpful if your dog is a traveler, like a beach-trip regular or a road-trip copilot. A finder in Destin who sees "Tampa" on the tag knows this dog is a long way from home.
- **Street name only.** Gives a finder a hint without the full picture.
- **Skip it entirely.** Totally fine if your number fits and is current.

## Rabies and License Tags

Separate from your ID tag, your dog may need to wear a rabies tag or a license tag, depending on where you live. Rules vary by state, county, and city. In Florida, rabies vaccination is required, and many counties also have licensing requirements. Check with your local county or vet so you know what applies to you.

These tags are official and they don't replace your ID tag. They have a number on them that *can* trace back to you, but usually only during business hours, through an office, after a few phone calls. Your ID tag is the fast lane. Use both.

## How Much Fits on a Dog Tag?

Less than you think. This is where most tag plans fall apart: someone writes a beautiful paragraph, then discovers they're working with a few short lines on something roughly the size of a quarter.

General guidance:

- **Keep lines short.** A handful of words per line reads best.
- **Use both sides.** Front for personality, back for contact info is a classic setup.
- **Prioritize ruthlessly.** Phone number first, critical medical info second, everything else after.
- **Bigger dog, bigger tag.** Our [bulldogs](https://saltydogtags.com/bulldog-dog-tags/) can carry a hefty tag without complaint. A Chihuahua probably shouldn't be wearing a dinner plate.

## Make It Last: Why Engraving Method Matters

A tag is only useful if a stranger can read it. Dogs scratch, dig, swim, roll in things you'd rather not identify, and drag their tags across every surface they meet. Salt water and sand are especially rough on gear. Florida beach dogs, this is about you.

That's why we laser-engrave our tags. Laser engraving gives you crisp, clean text that holds up to daily dog life far better than a cheap print that rubs off. Whatever you buy, ask how the text is applied. If a tag looks faded after a few weeks, it's not going to help much if your dog makes a break for it six months from now.

## Now for the Fun Part: Personality

Once the important stuff is covered, you've earned the right to be ridiculous. A funny tag doesn't just make you smile every time your dog trots by. It can make a stranger more likely to smile, laugh, and call. Nobody ignores a dog wearing a tag that says:

**Don't follow me.I'm lost too.**

Or the classic bulldog confession:

**Runs onnaps and spite.**

The best funny tags follow a simple rule: **setup, then punch.** Short line, shorter payoff. Some favorites from our catalog:

- **Not lost. Taking a tour.** For the dog who considers the fence a suggestion.
- **Return for reward. Reward is me.** Confidence, unearned.
- **Good boy (legally disputed).** For the dog with a record.
- **Does not come when called. Try snacks.** Funny *and* genuinely useful advice for a finder.
- **Velcro dog. Will not detach.** For the shadow that follows you to the bathroom.

Notice how some of these sneak in real info? "Try snacks" tells a stranger exactly how to catch your dog. That's the sweet spot: a joke that's also a tip.

And for the grown-ups with a saltier sense of humor, yes, we have an 18+ section. You know who you are. Just remember that kids, neighbors, and your vet's front desk will all read it eventually.

## A Simple Dog Tag Template

If you want a starting point, try this layout:

**Front:**

- Funny line (two or three short lines)

**Back:**

- Dog's name (optional)
- Phone number
- Second phone number or "Microchipped"
- Medical note (if needed)

That covers the essentials, leaves room for personality, and gives a stranger everything they need in about four seconds of reading.

## FAQ

Still stuck? [hello@saltydogtags.com](mailto:hello@saltydogtags.com). A human answers. The dogs supervise.

**What is the most important thing to put on a dog tag?**

Your phone number, with the area code. If you only have room for one thing, that's it. A finder can call you on the spot without needing a vet, shelter, or microchip scanner.

**Should I put my dog's name on the tag?**

It's a personal choice. A name can help calm a scared dog, but some owners leave it off out of theft concerns. If space is tight, prioritize your phone number over the name.

**Is it safe to put my address on a dog tag?**

Many people now skip the full address and rely on a phone number instead. If you want to include location info, a city or street name is a reasonable middle ground.

**Do I still need an ID tag if my dog is microchipped?**

Yes. A microchip requires a scanner, usually at a vet or shelter. A tag lets anyone contact you immediately. Put "Microchipped" on the tag so finders know there's a backup.

**Can a funny dog tag still be practical?**

Absolutely. Put your contact info on the back and the joke on the front. Some of the best funny tags, like "Does not come when called. Try snacks," actually give finders useful information.
