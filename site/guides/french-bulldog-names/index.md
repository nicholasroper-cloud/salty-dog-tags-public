## How to Pick the Right Name (From People Who Engrave Them for a Living)

A few practical tips from the engraving bench:

- **Shorter names read better on tags.** "Gigi" fits big and bold. "Sir Reginald Waffleton III" fits too, but in very small letters. Your call.
- **Say it out loud, angrily, in public.** You will yell this name at a dog park. Make sure you're okay with strangers hearing "TOOTER, NO."
- **Avoid names that sound like commands.** "Kit" sounds like "sit." "Bo" sounds like "no." Your dog will be confused, though honestly, they were going to ignore you anyway.
- **Give it a week.** Sometimes a dog grows into a name. Sometimes they make it obvious you got it wrong.

## FAQ

Still stuck? [hello@saltydogtags.com](mailto:hello@saltydogtags.com). A human answers. The dogs supervise.

**What's a good name for a French Bulldog?**

The best name fits your dog's personality. Grumpy loafs suit old-person names like Gerald or Edna. Wild ones suit Chaos or Riot. Round ones suit pastries. If you're stuck, watch your dog for a few days. They'll show you who they are, usually by destroying something.

**Should I give my Frenchie a French name?**

You can, and names like Pierre, Margot, and Bijou are a fun nod to the breed. But no rule says you have to. Plenty of great Frenchies are named Tank, Mango, or Doris. Your dog does not care where the name comes from. Your dog cares if the name sounds like "treat."

**Are short names better for dogs?**

Generally, yes. One- or two-syllable names are easier to call out and easier for dogs to pick up. They also engrave larger and cleaner on a tag, which matters when someone's squinting at your dog's collar while they wiggle.

**What should I put on my Frenchie's dog tag?**

At minimum: your dog's name and a phone number that you actually answer. Many people add "Microchipped" or a note about medical needs. After that, there's room for personality. A funny line on a tag doesn't replace the important info, but it does make the stranger who finds your dog smile while they dial.

**Do Frenchies do okay in Florida?**

They can live happily in Florida with smart care, but their short snouts make heat and humidity genuinely risky. Walk early or late, keep them in shade and air conditioning, always bring water, and skip midday beach trips. Name them Key Lime. Keep them cool.
