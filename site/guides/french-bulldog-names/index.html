100 Frenchie Names, Grouped by Theme (Because "Potato" Is Taken in Your Group Chat)

Salty Dog Tags · Updated Oct 13, 2026

You brought home a French Bulldog. Congratulations. You now own a small, loud, bat-eared loaf that snores like a leaf blower and believes your pillow is community property.

Now it needs a name.

We're a family in Lehi, Utah, with seven English bulldogs. Frenchies are their smaller, pointier-eared cousins who got the same snore in a travel size. We spend our days laser-engraving tags for dogs exactly like yours, so we've seen a lot of names. Some were great. Some were "Dog." We're not here to judge. We're here to help you do better than "Dog."

Below are 100 Frenchie names, grouped by theme. Pick one that fits your dog's personality, or the personality you're hoping they'll grow into. (They won't. But hope is free.)

Actually French Names (For the Purists)

Your dog is technically French. Lean in. These names work whether you're in Paris, Pensacola, or a Publix parking lot.

  1. Pierre: Sounds like he owns a vineyard. Owns a chew toy.
  2. Margot: Elegant. Will still eat a sock.
  3. Gigi: Short, sassy, fits on any tag.
  4. Remy: For the Frenchie who's definitely been in the kitchen.
  5. Colette: Classy enough for brunch, wild enough to steal yours.
  6. Jacques: Say it with a shrug.
  7. Lulu: Impossible to yell angrily. That's the point.
  8. Bijou: Means "jewel." Acts like one. Demands to be carried like one.
  9. Amélie: For the whimsical little weirdo.
  10. Gaston: Big personality. Bigger ego. Small legs.

Pastry and Snack Names (Because Look at Them)

Frenchies are shaped like baked goods. This is not an insult. It's a fact supported by every photo on your phone.

  1. Croissant: Curls up exactly like one.
  2. Brioche: Soft, round, slightly buttery-looking.
  3. Crêpe: Flat. Usually on the couch.
  4. Baguette: Ironic if your dog is a perfect circle.
  5. Macaron: Fancy, colorful, expensive to maintain.
  6. Éclair: Sweet outside, chaos filling.
  7. Beignet: A little French, a little New Orleans, all powdered-sugar energy.
  8. Truffle: Fancy. Also sniffs the ground constantly.
  9. Nugget: The universal small-dog name. Earned its spot.
  10. Waffle: For the Frenchie with wrinkles you could pour syrup into.

Florida Beach Names (Sun, Sand, and Strategic Shade)

Plenty of our tags end up on dogs living the Florida life. One honest note: Frenchies and heat do not get along. Those squished faces make cooling down hard, so beach days mean early mornings, shade, water, and an air-conditioned retreat. Name them after the beach. Let them enjoy it from under the umbrella.

  1. Key Lime: Tart. Sweet. Very Florida.
  2. Mango: Bright, juicy personality.
  3. Sandy: Will bring the beach home in every wrinkle.
  4. Coconut: Hard to crack. Soft inside.
  5. Gator: Tiny dog, big Florida energy.
  6. Pelican: Will dive for dropped food.
  7. Biscayne: Sounds like a yacht. Is a snorer.
  8. Sunny: For the optimist. Or the one who lies in sunbeams like a lizard.
  9. Marlin: Big fish name for a small dog with big-fish confidence.
  10. Flip Flop: Because that's the sound of them trotting down the hall.

Tiny Tough Guy Names (Irony Is a Love Language)

There's nothing funnier than a 22-pound dog named after a monster truck. Our English bulldogs back us up here. Nobody takes "Tank" seriously when he's asleep upside down.

  1. Tank: Built like one. Moves like a parked one.
  2. Brutus: Roman emperor energy. Bathtime meltdown energy.
  3. Bruiser: Bruises nothing. Bruised by a falling leaf.
  4. Moose: For the chunky boy.
  5. Diesel: Runs loud. Smells about right.
  6. Rocco: Sounds like he knows a guy.
  7. Duke: Small kingdom. Big attitude.
  8. Boss: He is not the boss. He thinks he is.
  9. Rambo: Afraid of the vacuum.
  10. Thor: God of thunder. Also god of under-the-blanket.

Old Person Names (Frenchies Are Born Retired)

French Bulldogs come out of the box acting like they've already done 40 years at the plant. Give them a name that matches their aura of a guy complaining about the thermostat.

  1. Gerald: Wants to see a manager.
  2. Doris: Has opinions about your shoes.
  3. Walter: Naps at 2. Naps at 3. Naps at 4.
  4. Mildred: Suspicious of strangers. And friends.
  5. Harold: Groans getting off the couch. Was on the couch for nine minutes.
  6. Bernice: Bridge club president. Treat club treasurer.
  7. Frank: Says exactly what he means. Usually with a snort.
  8. Gladys: Sweet as pie. Will still bite your ankle.
  9. Murray: Retired in Boca. Mentally.
  10. Edna: Strict. Loving. Will not be rushed.

How to Pick the Right Name (From People Who Engrave Them for a Living)

A few practical tips from the engraving bench:

  • Shorter names read better on tags. "Gigi" fits big and bold. "Sir Reginald Waffleton III" fits too, but in very small letters. Your call.
  • Say it out loud, angrily, in public. You will yell this name at a dog park. Make sure you're okay with strangers hearing "TOOTER, NO."
  • Avoid names that sound like commands. "Kit" sounds like "sit." "Bo" sounds like "no." Your dog will be confused, though honestly, they were going to ignore you anyway.
  • Give it a week. Sometimes a dog grows into a name. Sometimes they make it obvious you got it wrong.

Once you've got a name, pair it with a line that fits. Our Frenchie tags page has plenty for flat-faced dogs, and so does the bulldog page: "Snorts. Means it." "Built for comfort, not speed." "Velcro dog. Will not detach." The saying goes on the front; name and phone number go on the back. Questions humans ask

FAQ

Still stuck? hello@saltydogtags.com. A human answers. The dogs supervise.

What's a good name for a French Bulldog?

The best name fits your dog's personality. Grumpy loafs suit old-person names like Gerald or Edna. Wild ones suit Chaos or Riot. Round ones suit pastries. If you're stuck, watch your dog for a few days. They'll show you who they are, usually by destroying something.

Should I give my Frenchie a French name?

You can, and names like Pierre, Margot, and Bijou are a fun nod to the breed. But no rule says you have to. Plenty of great Frenchies are named Tank, Mango, or Doris. Your dog does not care where the name comes from. Your dog cares if the name sounds like "treat."

Are short names better for dogs?

Generally, yes. One- or two-syllable names are easier to call out and easier for dogs to pick up. They also engrave larger and cleaner on a tag, which matters when someone's squinting at your dog's collar while they wiggle.

What should I put on my Frenchie's dog tag?

At minimum: your dog's name and a phone number that you actually answer. Many people add "Microchipped" or a note about medical needs. After that, there's room for personality. A funny line on a tag doesn't replace the important info, but it does make the stranger who finds your dog smile while they dial.

Do Frenchies do okay in Florida?

They can live happily in Florida with smart care, but their short snouts make heat and humidity genuinely risky. Walk early or late, keep them in shade and air conditioning, always bring water, and skip midday beach trips. Name them Key Lime. Keep them cool.
