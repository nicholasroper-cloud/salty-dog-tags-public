## How to Choose a Dog Tag Size (Before Choosing a Breed)

Breed is a great starting point, but it's not the whole story. A few things matter more than what the shelter paperwork says:

**1. Your dog's neck and collar.** A tag should hang comfortably without smacking your dog in the chin or dragging around like an anchor. Look at the collar width and how much neck real estate you're working with.

**2. How much you want to say.** A short punchline like "Snorts. Means it." fits beautifully on a smaller tag. If your dog's personality requires a three-line setup and payoff, more surface area helps. Type the line into [the builder](https://saltydogtags.com/#build) and the preview shows what fits before you pay.

**3. Your dog's lifestyle.** Couch potato? Beach bum? Professional escape artist? A tag on a dog that's constantly digging, swimming, or sprinting through brush needs to be easy to read when a stranger finally catches them.

**4. Your dog's ego.** Some small dogs simply need a bigger tag. They know who they are. Respect it.

## Our Very Biased Bulldog Section

We have seven English bulldogs. We have opinions.

Bulldogs are a special sizing case because they're medium in height and heavyweight in everything else. Their necks are thick, their chests are broad, and their chins sit low, which means a tag hangs differently on them than on a leggy dog of similar weight.

For most adult English Bulldogs, **large (2 inch)** is the move. It's what we put on our own seven: big enough to read through the jowl-and-fold situation and proportional to that barrel body. A 1-inch tag disappears on a wide bulldog collar. Puppies and lighter-collared bulldogs can start on medium. More in our [bulldog tag guide](https://saltydogtags.com/bulldog-dog-tags/).

What we can tell you from daily life: bulldogs flop. They flop on the couch, flop on the kitchen tile, flop dramatically mid-walk because they've decided they're done. Whatever size you choose, make sure the tag hangs freely when your dog is lying in their natural habitat (horizontal).

**Bulldog-approved lines:**

- "I snore. It's not a medical thing."
- "Runs on naps and spite."
- "Can't swim. Still at the beach."
- "Snores louder than dad."

That last one is a household favorite. Accurate, too.

## What If My Dog Is Between Sizes?

Welcome to the club. Mixed breeds, lanky teenagers, and "the shelter said he'd stay small" dogs all live here.

A few quick rules of thumb:

- **Thin neck, light frame?** Size down.
- **Thick neck, stocky build?** Size up.
- **Long-haired or fluffy?** Size up, so the tag doesn't get lost in the floof.
- **Still growing?** Go with what fits now. You'll want a new tag eventually anyway, because your dog's personality will have evolved into something even more unhinged.
- **Can't decide?** Look at the collar. If the tag would look like a hubcap on it, size down. If it would look like a shirt button, size up.

## Puppies: Size for Now, Not Forever

Puppies grow fast. A Great Dane pup who fits a small tag today will need a large before you've finished house-training. That's fine. Get the tag that fits the dog in front of you, not the dog they'll become.

Bonus: you get to update the joke as they grow. A puppy wearing "Professional snuggler" might graduate to "Certified menace" by the time they've eaten their first shoe.

## FAQ

Still stuck? [hello@saltydogtags.com](mailto:hello@saltydogtags.com). A human answers. The dogs supervise.

**What size dog tag does my dog need?**

Start with breed as a general guide: small (1 inch, $12) for toy and small breeds, medium (1.5 inch, $14) for medium breeds, and large (2 inch, $16) for big dogs and stocky heavyweights like English bulldogs. Then adjust for neck thickness, coat, and how much text you want on the tag.

**What size tag is best for an English Bulldog?**

Large (2 inch) for most adult English Bulldogs. It's what we put on our own seven: their thick necks and wide collars make a smaller tag disappear. Puppies can start on medium.

**Can a small dog wear a medium tag?**

Sure, if it fits comfortably and doesn't bang into their chin or chest. Some stocky small breeds, like Pugs and Frenchies, look great in medium. Very tiny dogs, like Chihuahuas, are usually happier with a small.

**Does the tag size affect how much text I can fit?**

Generally, a bigger tag gives you more room. Short punchlines fit any size, while longer setups and multi-line jokes have more breathing room on medium or large. The builder preview shows exactly what fits.

**Should I buy a bigger tag for my puppy to grow into?**

We'd skip it. An oversized tag on a small puppy can be clunky and annoying for them. Get the size that fits now and upgrade later. Your dog's attitude will probably need a new tag by then anyway.
