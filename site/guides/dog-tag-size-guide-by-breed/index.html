Salty Dog Tags · Updated Oct 12, 2026

Here's the thing nobody tells you about dog tags: size matters. A tag that's too big on a tiny dog looks like a dinner plate, and a tag that's too small on a big dog disappears into the neck folds. Ask us how we know. We live with seven English bulldogs in Lehi, Utah, and between them there are more neck folds than a shar-pei convention.

So before you fall in love with a tag that says "Certified menace," let's make sure it actually fits the menace in question.

This guide walks through our three tag sizes (small, medium, and large), which breeds usually land where, and how to decide when your dog refuses to fit neatly into a category. Which, let's be honest, is most dogs.

How to Choose a Dog Tag Size (Before Choosing a Breed)

Breed is a great starting point, but it's not the whole story. A few things matter more than what the shelter paperwork says:

1. Your dog's neck and collar. A tag should hang comfortably without smacking your dog in the chin or dragging around like an anchor. Look at the collar width and how much neck real estate you're working with.

2. How much you want to say. A short punchline like "Snorts. Means it." fits beautifully on a smaller tag. If your dog's personality requires a three-line setup and payoff, more surface area helps. Type the line into the builder and the preview shows what fits before you pay.

3. Your dog's lifestyle. Couch potato? Beach bum? Professional escape artist? A tag on a dog that's constantly digging, swimming, or sprinting through brush needs to be easy to read when a stranger finally catches them.

4. Your dog's ego. Some small dogs simply need a bigger tag. They know who they are. Respect it.

Small Tags (1 inch, $12): Toy and Small Breeds

The small tag is built for the little ones: the lap dogs, the purse riders, the five-pound tyrants who run the whole house.

Breeds that usually fit a small tag:

  • Chihuahua
  • Yorkshire Terrier
  • Pomeranian
  • Maltese
  • Toy Poodle
  • Papillon
  • Shih Tzu
  • Miniature Dachshund (standards go Medium)
  • Italian Greyhound
  • Toy and miniature mixes

A 1-inch tag gives a tiny dog plenty of room to make a statement without dragging their whole head toward the floor. It's also a solid pick for puppies of nearly any breed while they grow into their paws and their attitude.

Tag lines that hit right on small dogs:

  • "Small dog. Big mouth."
  • "Small. Loud. Deeply loved."
  • "Velcro dog. Will not detach."

Small dogs are often the ones slipping through gaps in the fence or bolting out the door when the delivery guy shows up. A readable tag is a big deal for a dog that's easy to miss.

Medium Tags (1.5 inch, $14): The Sweet Spot

The medium tag is the middle child: dependable, easy to get along with, and secretly carrying the whole family. It works for a wide range of dogs, and it gives you a bit more room for those multi-line zingers.

Breeds that usually fit a medium tag:

  • French Bulldog
  • Pug
  • Beagle
  • Cavalier King Charles Spaniel
  • Cocker Spaniel
  • Corgi (Pembroke and Cardigan)
  • Boston Terrier
  • Border Collie
  • Australian Shepherd
  • Shiba Inu
  • Standard Schnauzer
  • Most medium-sized mixes

A note on Frenchies and Pugs: They're compact, but they're built like little bricks. Some owners go small, but many find a medium looks more proportional on that thick neck. If your dog is short but wide, medium is usually your friend.

Tag lines that hit right on medium dogs:

  • "Built for comfort, not speed."
  • "Return for reward. Reward is me."
  • "Does not come when called. Try snacks."

Our Very Biased Bulldog Section

We have seven English bulldogs. We have opinions.

Bulldogs are a special sizing case because they're medium in height and heavyweight in everything else. Their necks are thick, their chests are broad, and their chins sit low, which means a tag hangs differently on them than on a leggy dog of similar weight.

For most adult English Bulldogs, large (2 inch) is the move. It's what we put on our own seven: big enough to read through the jowl-and-fold situation and proportional to that barrel body. A 1-inch tag disappears on a wide bulldog collar. Puppies and lighter-collared bulldogs can start on medium. More in our bulldog tag guide.

What we can tell you from daily life: bulldogs flop. They flop on the couch, flop on the kitchen tile, flop dramatically mid-walk because they've decided they're done. Whatever size you choose, make sure the tag hangs freely when your dog is lying in their natural habitat (horizontal).

Bulldog-approved lines:

  • "I snore. It's not a medical thing."
  • "Runs on naps and spite."
  • "Can't swim. Still at the beach."
  • "Snores louder than dad."

That last one is a household favorite. Accurate, too.

The large tag (2 inch, $16) is for dogs who take up the whole bed and somehow still look offended when you ask for some space.

Breeds that usually fit a large tag:

  • English Bulldog
  • Labrador Retriever
  • Golden Retriever
  • German Shepherd
  • Boxer
  • Doberman Pinscher
  • Rottweiler
  • Siberian Husky
  • Bernese Mountain Dog
  • Great Dane
  • Goldendoodle and Labradoodle
  • Mastiff
  • Newfoundland
  • Saint Bernard
  • Large and giant mixes

On a big dog, a small tag tends to vanish into fur, especially on fluffy breeds like Bernese and Huskies. A large tag stays visible and gives a stranger an easier read when your 100-pound "lap dog" knocks them over to say hello.

Tag lines that hit right on big dogs:

  • "Good boy (legally disputed)."
  • "Outside is overrated. Take me home."
  • "I know I'm cute. Call my mom anyway."

What If My Dog Is Between Sizes?

Welcome to the club. Mixed breeds, lanky teenagers, and "the shelter said he'd stay small" dogs all live here.

A few quick rules of thumb:

  • Thin neck, light frame? Size down.
  • Thick neck, stocky build? Size up.
  • Long-haired or fluffy? Size up, so the tag doesn't get lost in the floof.
  • Still growing? Go with what fits now. You'll want a new tag eventually anyway, because your dog's personality will have evolved into something even more unhinged.
  • Can't decide? Look at the collar. If the tag would look like a hubcap on it, size down. If it would look like a shirt button, size up.

Puppies: Size for Now, Not Forever

Puppies grow fast. A Great Dane pup who fits a small tag today will need a large before you've finished house-training. That's fine. Get the tag that fits the dog in front of you, not the dog they'll become.

Bonus: you get to update the joke as they grow. A puppy wearing "Professional snuggler" might graduate to "Certified menace" by the time they've eaten their first shoe.

FAQ

Still stuck? hello@saltydogtags.com. A human answers. The dogs supervise.

What size dog tag does my dog need?

Start with breed as a general guide: small (1 inch, $12) for toy and small breeds, medium (1.5 inch, $14) for medium breeds, and large (2 inch, $16) for big dogs and stocky heavyweights like English bulldogs. Then adjust for neck thickness, coat, and how much text you want on the tag.

What size tag is best for an English Bulldog?

Large (2 inch) for most adult English Bulldogs. It's what we put on our own seven: their thick necks and wide collars make a smaller tag disappear. Puppies can start on medium.

Can a small dog wear a medium tag?

Sure, if it fits comfortably and doesn't bang into their chin or chest. Some stocky small breeds, like Pugs and Frenchies, look great in medium. Very tiny dogs, like Chihuahuas, are usually happier with a small.

Does the tag size affect how much text I can fit?

Generally, a bigger tag gives you more room. Short punchlines fit any size, while longer setups and multi-line jokes have more breathing room on medium or large. The builder preview shows exactly what fits.

Should I buy a bigger tag for my puppy to grow into?

We'd skip it. An oversized tag on a small puppy can be clunky and annoying for them. Get the size that fits now and upgrade later. Your dog's attitude will probably need a new tag by then anyway.
