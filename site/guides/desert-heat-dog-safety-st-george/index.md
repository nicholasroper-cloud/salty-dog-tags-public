## How hot it gets

Utah State Parks says daytime temperatures at Snow Canyon State Park **frequently top 105 degrees in July and August**. For people, the park's summer advice is to carry and drink water (32 ounces per person, per hour), wear sunscreen, dress for it, and **avoid the hotter hours, 11 a.m. to 7 p.m.**

Your dog doesn't read the sign, so plan its walks around the same hours.

Source: [Snow Canyon State Park current conditions](https://stateparks.utah.gov/parks/snow-canyon/current-conditions/).

## The pavement test

Zion National Park's B.A.R.K. Ranger page puts it simply: "Hot to touch? Too hot for paws!" It says to avoid walking your pet in the heat of the day and gives a **five-second rule**: place the back of your hand on the pavement, and if you can't hold it there for five seconds, it's too hot to walk your pet.

Around town that means sidewalks, parking lots and paved trails. Do the test before you leave the car, not halfway down the block.

Source: [Zion National Park, B.A.R.K. Ranger](https://home.nps.gov/articles/000/zionbarkranger.htm).

## Never in the car

The National Weather Service is blunt: **don't leave pets in cars.** Even in cool temperatures, cars can heat up to dangerous temperatures very quickly, and **even with the windows cracked, the temperature inside can rise almost 20 degrees Fahrenheit in the first 10 minutes.** Any pet left inside is at risk of serious heat-related illness or death.

Zion's pets page says the same about the desert: during most of the year, the inside of a vehicle can quickly warm to dangerous levels. If the plan includes a stop where the dog can't come in, the dog stays home. The park lists boarding kennels in Hurricane, St. George, Kanab, Cedar City and Rockville for park days.

Sources: [National Weather Service pet safety](https://www.weather.gov/mqt/petsafety) and [Zion National Park: pets](https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/pets.htm).

## On the trail: water, water quality, wildlife, weather

- **Water:** "Always carry extra food and water for your pet," says Zion's B.A.R.K. Ranger page. Ask a ranger if you need the closest drinking water.
- **Not that water:** in Zion, keep dogs out of the Virgin River. The park monitors it for toxic cyanobacteria, which can be present at any time of year, and dogs may ingest it.
- **Leash on:** Snow Canyon (6 feet max) and the Red Cliffs Desert Reserve both require a leash. Both warn about rattlesnakes and Gila monsters, and Snow Canyon adds scorpions and spiders. Rules for each place are in our [dog parks and trails guide](https://saltydogtags.com/guides/st-george-dog-parks-and-trails/#snow-canyon).
- **Monsoon:** Snow Canyon says the summer monsoon season usually runs July through September, with the potential for heavy afternoon rain and flash floods.

Sources: [Zion B.A.R.K. Ranger](https://home.nps.gov/articles/000/zionbarkranger.htm), [Zion: pets](https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/pets.htm), [Snow Canyon State Park](https://stateparks.utah.gov/parks/snow-canyon/) and [Red Cliffs Desert Reserve rules](https://www.redcliffsdesertreserve.com/rules).

## If your dog gets loose in the heat

This is where the tag earns its spot on the collar. A loose dog on a July afternoon needs to get home quickly, and shelters keep office hours:

- **St. George Animal Shelter:** by appointment only, Monday to Saturday; closed Sunday. 435-627-4350. ([city page](https://www.sgcityutah.gov/departments/police_department/animal_services___shelter.php))
- **Washington City Animal Shelter:** Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 4 p.m.; closed weekends and holidays. (435) 673-7194. ([city page](https://washingtonutah.gov/services/publicsafety/police/animalwelfare))
- **Hurricane Animal Shelter:** 435-635-8314. ([city page](https://www.cityofhurricane.com/159/Animal-Control-Shelter))
- **Santa Clara–Ivins Animal Control:** 435-628-1049. ([Ivins Public Safety](https://ivinsutah.gov/public-safety-3/))

A finder who can read your phone number on the tag can call you at 2 p.m. on a Sunday. A finder who can only read a license number has to wait for an office to open. So:

- **Phone number first and biggest**, then the dog's name. A second number helps if you hike where your phone has no signal.
- **Engraved**, so the number is cut into the metal rather than printed on top. Our guide to [whether dog tags wear off](https://saltydogtags.com/guides/do-dog-tags-wear-off/) compares the methods.
- **Keep the license tag current too.** In St. George, renewals are due by February 28; our [license rules guide](https://saltydogtags.com/guides/st-george-dog-license-rules/) has every city's dates.

Optional backup: on tags that support it, a $5 scan-to-find QR on the back opens a finder page for your dog, with email alerts to you once you activate it. Keep the number engraved anyway. A QR needs a signal, and trails don't always have one.

## Quick summer checklist

- Walk early or late, and do the five-second pavement test first.
- Never leave the dog in the car, windows cracked or not.
- Carry water for the dog on every trail, and keep it out of the Virgin River in Zion.
- Use a 6-foot leash on Snow Canyon and Red Cliffs trails.
